The island's UI: five ideas

Today's screen first, then five ways it could go, drawn over the same island. All keep the look you like: paper, IM Fell small caps, the hand in blue. In every idea:

  • the enclosure tags shrink to small numbered seals; the full tag (number and size) shows on hover, or when zoomed in close;
  • the FPS counter is gone (a developer's readout);
  • "back to the journal" is no longer a bold link of its own, but a quiet way out tucked into the island's name.

Now

What reads a little busy: the enclosure tags crowd the middle; the top left mixes three shapes (a tilted name tag, a pill link, small square tags) and the buttons are small and pale; the top right pairs a rectangle with a rounded pill.

1. Tidy corners

The smallest change. The name tag gets a folded corner with a small arrow: that is the way back to the journal (it reads "back to the journal" on hover). Under it, build, weather and bestiary become three tags of one size, a touch larger. On the right, one plate for the Fenmarks and the island's time, a fine rule between.

GoodFamiliar, almost nothing moves; every corner speaks one shape.
Less goodStill two busy corners at the top; the map's top edge stays covered.

2. Bookmark ribbons

The tools hang from the top edge like the ribbon bookmarks of the journal: rust for build, blue for weather, moss for the bestiary, each with its sign. The island's name sits centred at the top on a small engraved plate, with a tiny "journal" tab at its left end.

GoodVery much the journal's own idea; colour tells the tools apart at a glance.
Less goodRibbons take height at the top; on a phone they need to shrink to tabs.

3. A tray below

The tools move to one paper tray at the bottom centre, larger, with the weather written in the tray ("autumn · night · light rain"). Cards open upward from it. Top left keeps only a slim name tag with a small arrow at its left: the way back. The top of the map is left almost clear.

GoodThe tools sit where thumbs and eyes rest; the map reads widest; works well on phones.
Less goodA creature's card and the tray share the bottom: the card moves to the right.

4. The journal's margin

A slim paper margin down the left edge, ruled with the journal's red line: the island's name at the top, the three tools stacked with their names, the island's time at the foot, and below it in small hand "back to the journal". The rest of the map is free; the Fenmarks sit alone at the top right.

GoodEverything in one calm place; the clearest sense of "a page of the journal, the island beside it".
Less goodTakes a strip of the map at all times (it could fold away to its edge).

5. Medallions

The most game-like: a brass seal at the top left carries the island's number; tap it and the tools fan out round it as small tokens (build, weather, bestiary, and the journal). At the top right a pocket watch shows the island's time, the Fenmarks on a tag hung from its chain.

GoodAlmost nothing on screen until wanted; strong character, like a game's own instruments.
Less goodThe tools are one tap further away; the names show only when the fan is open.
